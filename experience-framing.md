# Career Arc

Flash and ActionScript gave way to standards-based web. Static front-end work became untenable once JavaScript frameworks took over, too much to maintain alongside being a strong UX designer, so the focus sharpened. Now AI is shifting the medium again. # Design Systems Point of View

## The Two-Path Framework
When starting a design system now, there are two reasonable approaches:

**Path one, adopt and remap:** Find a Tailwind-based out-of-the-box library (shadcn, etc.), update the Tailwind variables to match brand. Right for most SaaS work where the UI patterns are largely solved. These libraries also have LLM-optimized versions that work well with AI-assisted development.

**Path two, build from foundations with AI:** Start with basic brand elements (colors, type scale), use AI to extrapolate tints, shades, and type rhythms. Match Tailwind constraints. Seed with a few custom components and let AI extrapolate into code first, then port to Figma artifacts for exploration. Code is the source of truth from the start.

## The Elevate Caveat
Elevate was a real accomplishment, it redefined the visual and component foundation across all G2 products and had meaningful impact on design team velocity. But it was built too close to the Rails environment to age well into AI-assisted development workflows. A colleague rebuilt a lighter version with Claude in a fraction of the time.

The lesson is to keep implementation, tokens, and readable context aligned, with Figma supporting exploration. Describe the colleague's rebuild as context for an evolving approach, not a quantified productivity result.

Don't over-tell the Elevate story. It's table stakes territory. If it comes up, say plainly how the thinking has evolved.

# G2, Experience Framing

## The Real Frame
The G2 story isn't one hero case study. It's a pattern of influence: introducing ideas early through speculative and vision work, proving them through execution, and creating foundations other people found useful enough to build on without being asked. Measure success by adoption, not just by launch.

## Inline Editing
Started as a speculative concept in visual narrative explorations, what would it look like if sellers could edit their profile page directly on the page, with an AI assistant helping? That work lived in animatics and prototype territory for a while. Later implemented for real with the Solutions product: first on the seller profile page, then on the Solutions page itself. Once proven buildable with a code-based framework, the squad responsible for seller profile pages adopted it independently and extended it further.

The success is the handoff. They didn't need to be asked. That's the point.

In interviews: "One thing I care about is whether my work creates leverage for other designers. Inline editing is a good example. It started as a speculative prototype, became a real pattern in Solutions, and then another squad picked it up and extended it. I didn't have to advocate for it. They just found it useful."

## AI-Generated Draft States
Designed and helped implement the ability to generate a fully launchable draft Solutions page from just a title and a product selection. AI handled the rest. That pattern started showing up elsewhere in the product. Same dynamic: introduce it somewhere, prove it works, let it propagate.

## Onboarding Patterns
Patterns developed for onboarding wizards were adopted by other designers and showed up in different products and squads, not through advocacy, but because they were useful.

## Solutions Product, Concrete Case Study
This is the one story with a clean arc and real numbers. Use it as the primary G2 case study.

Problem: Suites (multi-product bundles) wasn't working. Buyers didn't understand it, sellers couldn't articulate the value, churn was high.

Reframe: Sellers had no way to communicate their multi-product value on G2. Buyers had no way to understand it. Needed a flexible, modular way to tell a solution-level story.

Process: 4-week innovation sprints with clear goals. Rapid testing and iteration without losing sight of the north star. Stepping stones included on-page editing features, featured products section, and solution filters before the full product launched.

Results: $3M attributed revenue (3x the original goal), 30+ Solution Pages created, 20% increase in buyer engagement for sellers who create Solutions, cornerstone of the new Enterprise package.

## What to Watch Out For
Buyer intent work felt unfinished at departure. Keep it vague or omit it. Hard to tell well, and the outcome is uncertain given the acquisition context.
